Proposed Thesis · Scripps Institution of Oceanography, UC San Diego
Applying Deep Learning to Coastal Ecosystem Moniotring and Accelerated Recovery with Audio-Visual Approaches
Oceane Boulais Advisor: TBD Expected Defense — December 2026
Previously Proposed Thesis Overview
Chapter Breakdown
Chapter 1
Validates acoustic playback as a restoration tool by demonstrating how it increases fish larval presence on artificial coral reef structures near new-moon phases, while establishing autonomous cameras for evaluating ecological restoration tools in situ.
Chapter 2
Develops a novel automated pipeline combining particle tracking velocimetry (PTV) with particle image velocimetry (PIV) to distinguish actively swimming larvae from drifting particles, enabling reproducible abundance estimates in challenging field conditions with turbidity and variable lighting.
Chapter 3
Applies deep learning to seven years of passive acoustic data to detect and characterize bowhead whale call types, revealing how calling behavior varies with environmental factors like seismic airgun noise.
Chapter 4
Introduces a multimodal, information-theoretic framework using directional acoustic data to quantitatively compare artificial and natural reefs over time.
